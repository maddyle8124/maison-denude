# Cognitive Bias Glossary

_Reference for the daily-debrief skill. Used during Phase 3 — Bias Detection._

---

## Biases Relevant to Product/Business Decisions

### Confirmation Bias
**What it is:** Seeking or interpreting information in a way that confirms existing beliefs.
**Trigger patterns:** "I knew this would work", "everyone I talked to agrees", "the data backs up what I already thought"
**Reframe question:** "What evidence would change your mind on this? Have you actively looked for it?"

### Sunk Cost Fallacy
**What it is:** Continuing a course of action because of previously invested resources, not because it's the right path forward.
**Trigger patterns:** "We've already spent so much time on this", "I can't abandon it now after all this work"
**Reframe question:** "If you were starting fresh today with no prior investment, would you still choose this path?"

### Optimism Bias
**What it is:** Overestimating positive outcomes and underestimating risks or obstacles.
**Trigger patterns:** "This will definitely work", "users will love this", "we'll hit X by end of month" (without evidence)
**Reframe question:** "What's the realistic downside scenario? What does it look like if this takes 3x longer than expected?"

### Planning Fallacy
**What it is:** Underestimating the time, costs, and risks of future actions.
**Trigger patterns:** "This will only take a week", "it's a simple feature", "we can do this in parallel"
**Reframe question:** "What's the last time a similar task took as long as you expected? What made it harder than planned?"

### Availability Heuristic
**What it is:** Overweighting recent or vivid examples when making decisions.
**Trigger patterns:** "I just talked to one person who said X, so that must be what users want", "I saw this work for [other product]"
**Reframe question:** "Is this one data point representative? What would a broader sample look like?"

### Anchoring Bias
**What it is:** Over-relying on the first piece of information encountered when making decisions.
**Trigger patterns:** Accepting the first number/timeline/scope without questioning, "that's the standard price so..."
**Reframe question:** "If the first number you heard had been 2x higher or lower, would your thinking be different?"

### Local/Insider Bias (context-specific)
**What it is:** Assuming your personal experience as a local is universal to all locals, or that what you find valuable is what users want.
**Trigger patterns:** "Every local knows this", "people here don't use X", "Vietnamese people prefer Y"
**Reframe question:** "Is this true for all locals or just your social circle? Have you validated this with people outside your network?"

### False Consensus Effect
**What it is:** Overestimating how much others share your beliefs, behaviors, or preferences.
**Trigger patterns:** "Everyone wants this", "expats all agree that...", "it's obvious that users need X"
**Reframe question:** "What percentage of your target users have you actually talked to about this? What did the others say?"

### Scope Creep Rationalization
**What it is:** Justifying scope expansion with "just one more thing" logic that delays the core product.
**Trigger patterns:** "We should also add...", "while we're at it, let's...", "it would be easy to include..."
**Reframe question:** "If you could only launch one thing, what would it be? Does this addition make that one thing better or distract from it?"

---

## How to Surface a Bias (reminder)

1. Name it non-judgmentally: "Mình muốt lưu ý một điều — cái này có thể là [tên bias]."
2. Quote the specific trigger: "Lúc bạn nói '[quote]'..."
3. Offer the reframe question above.
4. Accept whatever the user says — do not push further.
5. Log it in the session file regardless of user agreement.
